## What the frame-by-frame says about YOUR filming

1. **One long take.** Your 18 Jul Reel had 1 cut in 46 s. 18 Sep had 1 cut in 45 s.
   Farah cuts every 3–5 s. On a phone, a static frame for 20 s is where
   people swipe away. **Fix:** cut every sentence. Jump cuts are fine. Zoom in
   10–15% on every second cut to fake a second camera angle.
2. **The hook arrives too late.** The analyser measured your first shot at
   17–34 s on 4 of 6 videos. The one with a 3-second bold hook (11 Jul, "if she
   knows she's beautiful, run") got 10x your usual comments. **Fix:** say the
   most surprising sentence first, in under 3 s, and put it on screen as text.
3. **Always in the car, always alone.** It's consistent, but the viewer sees
   the same frame every time and learns nothing about your life. Farah's
   frames change every few seconds: her room, outfits, event venues, a WhatsApp
   screenshot from her mum.
4. **Sound:** it's your voice only, with no music bed. That's OK for advice, but
   car audio picks up road and AC noise, and the analyser can't measure that.
   **Fix:** a cheap clip-on mic (Boya / Rode Wireless Me, about 1,000–2,500 EGP),
   AC off while recording, and a quiet music bed at about 10% under your voice.
5. **Length:** your 84–85 s Reels are your weakest. Your two best are 27 s and
   46 s. **Target 25–40 s.**
6. **Captions:** partial and small. Farah puts every key phrase on screen,
   because most people watch muted. **Fix:** CapCut auto-captions in Arabic, big,
   middle of the screen, 2–4 words at a time.

## Why Farah gets invited to events (and why you don't, yet)

Her events don't come from the follower count alone. They come from **being
useful inside the event and filming it**. She builds the content pipeline for
Longines, El Gouna and Rise Up, then posts "this is the system I built for
them". The organisers see her as part of the event, not a guest.

Her reach comes from a completely different kind of post: relatable Egyptian
comedy (the mum texting "where are you?", the flooded beach, "tidy the room
before your girlfriend comes"). Those get 2–6 **million** views and bring
in followers. Then the work videos turn those followers into respect.

**The formula:** relatable daily-life humour for reach + "I built this for
[event]" for authority + showing up on camera at the event = invitations.

You have the same raw materials: you're funny (the May–June skits), you
build AI automations, and you're in Cairo. Right now you're using none
of them on camera. You're giving generic advice that thousands of other
accounts also give.

## What to do differently: the unconventional path

You asked for something that breaks the usual rules. These are bold moves,
not risky ones. Buying followers, bots, follow/unfollow and engagement pods
get accounts shadowbanned, and organisers check engagement, so they would
hurt your goal.

1. **Build first, ask later.** Pick an upcoming Cairo tech or creative event
   (RiseUp, Techne Summit, a GDG / AI meetup, a design week). Build them
   something small and real for free: an AI registration assistant, an
   auto-recap generator, a speaker FAQ bot. Film yourself building it ("Day 1
   of building an AI system for [event] that they didn't ask for"), then DM
   it to the organiser. Either they use it (you're inside the event) or the
   series itself goes viral. You win either way.
2. **The "Interior Architect → AI" series.** This is the story nobody else
   has. Use your architecture eye: "I redesigned this café's ordering with AI",
   "Architecture taught me X about building automations". This is the bio,
   finally shown on camera.
3. **30-day daily vlog sprint, like Farah.** One Reel a day for 30 days about
   your real day: what you built, who you met, a fail, a win. The format can
   be rough. Consistency is the signal the algorithm rewards.
4. **Keep the comedy.** Your skits (May–June) and your bold 3-second hooks are
   your reach engine. One funny relatable Reel a week about AI or work life in
   Egypt, in your Franco voice.
5. **Show up and film at every event**, even as a normal attendee. Post the
   same day, tag the organisers and speakers, and say one sharp takeaway. Then
   comment thoughtfully on the organisers' posts every week.
6. **Collab with people one step ahead** (5k–50k followers in Egyptian tech).
   Offer to automate something for them on camera.
